\begin{abstract}
Despite advances in post-pretraining safety alignment---ranging from Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to multi-level safety constraints (Safe-RLHF)---Large Language Models (LLMs) remain vulnerable to adversarial prompt injections and jailbreaks (e.g., GCG suffixes, AutoDAN mutations, PAIR roleplay, and indirect context overrides). We trace this fragility to \textit{latent representation entanglement}: standard Transformer residual streams couple underlying task \textit{intent} with surface \textit{contextual framing} (syntax, persona wrappers, and rhetorical style). Adversaries exploit the context dimensions to mask harmful intent from linear safety boundaries, while existing activation-steering defenses indiscriminately perturb context features, inducing severe false refusals on benign dual-use queries.

To address this tension, we propose \textbf{ARETE} (\textbf{A}daptive \textbf{R}iemannian \textbf{E}thical \textbf{T}opology \textbf{E}nforcement), a geometric activation-intervention framework comprising three components: (1) \textbf{Bundle-Orthogonal Intent-Context Decomposition}, which maps intermediate residual states $h \in \mathbb{R}^D$ into radial norms and directional coordinates on unit Riemannian hyperspheres ($\mathbb{S}^{d_I-1} \times \mathbb{S}^{d_C-1}$), achieving empirical geodesic intent invariance ($\epsilon_{\text{mean}} = 0.1596$ rad on controlled manifolds and $0.2368$ rad at Layer 14 of \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}); (2) a 19-category \textbf{Geodesic Safety Barrier (GSB)} aligned with the PKU-SafeRLHF harm taxonomy that performs exact single-step spherical-cap retraction; and (3) \textbf{Subspace Orthogonal Null-Space Steering}, which projects defensive corrections onto the orthogonal complement $\mathcal{P}_{\text{context}}^\perp$ of the context subspace. Across five attack paradigms, out-of-distribution (OOD) unseen attack splits, and white-box adaptive PGD attacks, ARETE reduces Attack Success Rate (ASR) to $0.17\%$ in-distribution (95\% Clopper-Pearson CI: $[0.004\%, 0.92\%]$), $1.33\%$ on unseen attack families, and $2.82\%$ under adaptive white-box PGD, while maintaining $0.0\%$ benign false-positive refusals and preserving exact benign generation fluency on \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The deployment of Large Language Models (LLMs) across autonomous workflows, software engineering assistants, and enterprise decision support has made robust safety adherence a core requirement \citep{christiano2017deep,ouyang2022training,bai2022constitutional,ji2023beavertails}. Alignment pipelines---including RLHF \citep{ouyang2022training}, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) \citep{rafailov2023direct}, and decoupled harmlessness-helpfulness constrained optimization (Safe-RLHF) \citep{dai2024safe,ji2024pkusaferlhf,ji2025saferlhfv}---substantially reduce harmful generations on standard conversational distributions.

However, behavioral alignment remains fragile against adversarial framing and prompt injections \citep{wei2024jailbroken,zou2023universal,mazeika2024harmbench}. Attackers routinely bypass refusal boundaries using automated Greedy Coordinate Gradient (GCG) token suffixes \citep{zou2023universal}, hierarchical genetic mutations (AutoDAN) \citep{liu2023autodan}, iterative LLM-driven roleplay refinement (PAIR) \citep{chao2023jailbreaking}, multi-turn Crescendo escalations \citep{russinovich2024crescendo}, and indirect third-party document injections. Across these paradigms, the core malicious objective (e.g., synthesizing exploit payloads or wiping forensic logs) is preserved while its rhetorical or syntactic wrapper is heavily shifted.

Current post-hoc defenses fall into two categories, each facing distinct trade-offs:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{External Input/Output Guardrails and Decoding Filters}: Moderation classifiers such as Llama Guard \citep{inan2023llama}, Beaver-Guard \citep{ji2025saferlhfv}, SmoothLLM \citep{robey2023smoothllm}, and SafeDecoding \citep{xu2024safedecoding} inspect text or logits externally. These methods incur extra inference latency and remain vulnerable to indirect prompt injections and obfuscated framing.
    \item \textbf{Linear Activation Steering and Conditional Probing}: Internal representation interventions---including Representation Engineering (RepEng) \citep{zou2023representation}, 1D refusal-direction addition \citep{arditi2024refusal}, Circuit Breakers \citep{zou2024circuit}, and Conditional Activation Steering (CAST) \citep{lee2024programming}---shift hidden states along a refusal vector. However, operating directly on entangled residual streams causes two failure modes: (i) \textit{exaggerated over-refusal} on benign dual-use prompts containing sensitive keywords (e.g., ``How do I kill a stuck background process in Linux?''), as documented by XSTest \citep{rottger2023xstest} and OR-Bench \citep{cui2024orbench}; and (ii) \textit{contextual collateral damage}, where adding an unconstrained steering vector corrupts surrounding syntactic and contextual features.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Our Approach: ARETE.}
We address these failure modes by formulating safety enforcement as a \textit{Riemannian spherical-cap boundary problem} over disentangled intent and context representations. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Bundle-Orthogonal Intent-Context Decomposition}: We decompose residual states $h \in \mathbb{R}^D$ into radial norms $r(h) = \|h\|_2$ and unit hyperspherical coordinates $(z_{\text{intent}}, z_{\text{context}}) \in \mathbb{S}^{d_I-1} \times \mathbb{S}^{d_C-1}$. We derive an exact chord-margin safety bound (Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance}) linking geodesic intent stability to spherical-cap detection under adversarial perturbations.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Category Geodesic Safety Barrier (GSB)}: We construct 19 calibrated spherical-cap boundaries aligned with the PKU-SafeRLHF taxonomy \citep{ji2024pkusaferlhf} and derive the closed-form geodesic metric retraction $\pi_{\mathcal{K}_{k^*}}(z_{\text{intent}})$ onto the safe spherical cap in a single step (Proposition~\ref{prop:retraction}).
    \item \textbf{Subspace Orthogonal Null-Space Steering}: We prove (Theorem~\ref{thm:nullspace}) that projecting lifted steering vectors onto the orthogonal complement $\mathcal{P}_{\text{context}}^\perp$ of both the global rank-$r_C$ context subspace and sample-specific context vector guarantees zero linear perturbation to the context subspace while leaving benign queries untouched ($\Delta h \equiv \mathbf{0}$).
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive Empirical \& Real-LLM Validation}: We evaluate ARETE on controlled manifold benchmarks (including OOD unseen attack families and 10-step white-box adaptive PGD attacks) and on real Layer-14 activations and autoregressive generations of \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}, demonstrating strong jailbreak suppression without benign over-refusal.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Safety Alignment and Taxonomies.}
Post-pretraining alignment methods train LLMs via RLHF \citep{christiano2017deep,ouyang2022training,bai2022constitutional} or DPO \citep{rafailov2023direct}. Safe-RLHF \citep{dai2024safe}, BeaverTails \citep{ji2023beavertails}, and PKU-SafeRLHF \citep{ji2024pkusaferlhf} decouple helpfulness and harmlessness via constrained optimization across 19 granular harm categories and three severity levels, recently extended to multimodal inputs in Safe RLHF-V \citep{ji2025saferlhfv}. Despite strong average-case alignment, weight-space preference tuning remains susceptible to distribution shifts induced by adversarial framing \citep{wei2024jailbroken,carlini2019evaluating,tramer2020adaptive}.

\paragraph{Adversarial Jailbreaks.}
Automated jailbreaks systematically evade safety boundaries. GCG \citep{zou2023universal} optimizes discrete token suffixes via coordinate gradients; AutoDAN \citep{liu2023autodan} applies hierarchical genetic mutations to preserve fluency; PAIR \citep{chao2023jailbreaking} uses an attacker LLM to iteratively refine persona wrappers; and Crescendo \citep{russinovich2024crescendo} escalates across multi-turn dialogues. Standardized suites such as JailbreakBench \citep{chao2024jailbreakbench} and HarmBench \citep{mazeika2024harmbench} confirm that both open-weight and frontier models remain vulnerable.

\paragraph{Representation Engineering and Activation Steering.}
Internal activation methods intervene directly on Transformer hidden states \citep{zou2023representation}. \citet{arditi2024refusal} showed that refusal behavior is mediated by a dominant 1D direction, while Circuit Breakers \citep{zou2024circuit} reroute harmful representations during training. To reduce false refusals on benchmarks such as XSTest \citep{rottger2023xstest} and OR-Bench \citep{cui2024orbench}, Conditional Activation Steering (CAST) \citep{lee2024programming} gates a 1D refusal vector using a linear activation condition. However, existing methods operate on raw, entangled hidden states and apply rank-1 shifts without disentangling intent from context or projecting out the context subspace. ARETE advances this line of work by combining non-linear hyperspherical intent-context decomposition with 19-category geodesic cap retraction and subspace null-space projection.

\section{Theoretical Framework}
\label{sec:formulation}

\subsection{Hyperspherical Intent-Context Decomposition}
Let $h \in \mathbb{R}^D \setminus \{\mathbf{0}\}$ denote the residual stream hidden state at intermediate Transformer layer $l$ (at the last prompt token position during prefill). In standard Transformers, semantic task intent and rhetorical framing are non-linearly coupled within $h$. We introduce two non-linear two-layer SiLU encoders $f_I: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^{d_I}$ and $f_C: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}^{d_C}$ ($d_I + d_C = D$), followed by LayerNorm ($\text{LN}$) and $\ell_2$ normalization onto unit Riemannian hyperspheres \citep{absil2008optimization,bronstein2021geometric}:
\begin{align}
    z_{\text{intent}} &= \phi_I(h) = \frac{\text{LN}(f_I(h))}{\|\text{LN}(f_I(h))\|_2} \in \mathbb{S}^{d_I-1}, \label{eq:phi_I} \\
    z_{\text{context}} &= \phi_C(h) = \frac{\text{LN}(f_C(h))}{\|\text{LN}(f_C(h))\|_2} \in \mathbb{S}^{d_C-1}. \label{eq:phi_C}
\end{align}
To preserve the radial magnitude discarded by spherical normalization, we retain $r(h) = \|h\|_2 \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ and define a partitioned linear reconstruction map $\Phi = [\mathbf{W}_I, \mathbf{W}_C] \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times (d_I + d_C)}$:
\begin{equation}
    h_{\text{recon}} = r(h)\,\Phi\big([z_{\text{intent}}; z_{\text{context}}]\big) = r(h)\left(\mathbf{W}_I z_{\text{intent}} + \mathbf{W}_C z_{\text{context}}\right).
    \label{eq:recon}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Adversarial Perturbations \& Chord-Margin Safety Bound}
Let $\mathcal{T}_{\text{adv}}$ denote a family of intent-preserving adversarial framing transformations (e.g., GCG suffixes, PAIR roleplay wrappers, AutoDAN mutations, or indirect context injections). Equipped with the canonical Riemannian metric on $\mathbb{S}^{d_I-1}$, the geodesic distance between $u, v \in \mathbb{S}^{d_I-1}$ is $d_g(u, v) = \arccos(\langle u, v \rangle) \in [0, \pi]$.

\begin{definition}[$(\epsilon, \delta)$-Adversarial Intent Stability]
\label{def:invariance}
An intent encoder $\phi_I: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{S}^{d_I-1}$ satisfies $(\epsilon, \delta)$-adversarial intent stability under $\mathcal{T}_{\text{adv}}$ if, for $x \sim \mathcal{D}$ and $\tau \in \mathcal{T}_{\text{adv}}$, the geodesic divergence $D_g(x, \tau) = d_g\big(\phi_I(h(x)), \phi_I(h(\tau(x)))\big)$ satisfies $\mathbb{E}[D_g(x, \tau)] \le \epsilon_{\text{mean}}$ and $\mathbb{P}\big(D_g(x, \tau) \le \epsilon\big) \ge 1 - \delta$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Spherical-Cap Safety Preservation Under $\epsilon$-Perturbation]
\label{prop:invariance}
Let $z_{\text{clean}} = \phi_I(h(x))$ and $z_{\text{adv}} = \phi_I(h(\tau(x)))$ satisfy $d_g(z_{\text{clean}}, z_{\text{adv}}) \le \epsilon$ for $\epsilon \in [0, \pi]$. For any harm category $k$ with unit boundary normal $w_k \in \mathbb{S}^{d_I-1}$ and cap threshold $\gamma_k \in (0, 1)$, define the violation margin $m_k(z) = \langle z, w_k \rangle - \gamma_k$. If the clean query violates boundary $k$ with margin exceeding the chord bound:
\begin{equation}
    m_k(z_{\text{clean}}) > 2\sin(\epsilon / 2),
\end{equation}
then the adversarially cloaked query satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    m_k(z_{\text{adv}}) \ge m_k(z_{\text{clean}}) - 2\sin(\epsilon / 2) > 0,
\end{equation}
and more tightly, by the spherical law of cosines, $m_k(z_{\text{adv}}) \ge \cos(\epsilon)\langle z_{\text{clean}}, w_k \rangle - \sin(\epsilon)\sqrt{1 - \langle z_{\text{clean}}, w_k \rangle^2} - \gamma_k$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $\theta = d_g(z_{\text{clean}}, z_{\text{adv}}) \le \epsilon$. Since $z_{\text{clean}}, z_{\text{adv}} \in \mathbb{S}^{d_I-1}$, their Euclidean chord distance in $\mathbb{R}^{d_I}$ is $\|z_{\text{adv}} - z_{\text{clean}}\|_2 = \sqrt{2 - 2\cos\theta} = 2\sin(\theta/2) \le 2\sin(\epsilon/2)$, as $t \mapsto \sin(t/2)$ is strictly increasing on $[0, \pi]$. By Cauchy-Schwarz and $\|w_k\|_2 = 1$:
\begin{equation*}
    \big|\langle z_{\text{adv}}, w_k \rangle - \langle z_{\text{clean}}, w_k \rangle\big| \le \|z_{\text{adv}} - z_{\text{clean}}\|_2 \|w_k\|_2 \le 2\sin(\epsilon/2).
\end{equation*}
Subtracting $\gamma_k$ yields $m_k(z_{\text{adv}}) \ge m_k(z_{\text{clean}}) - 2\sin(\epsilon/2) > 0$. For the tighter trigonometric bound, by the triangle inequality for geodesic distance on $\mathbb{S}^{d_I-1}$, $d_g(z_{\text{adv}}, w_k) \le d_g(z_{\text{clean}}, w_k) + \theta \le \arccos(\langle z_{\text{clean}}, w_k \rangle) + \epsilon$; taking cosines via $\cos(\alpha + \epsilon) = \cos\alpha\cos\epsilon - \sin\alpha\sin\epsilon$ yields the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Geodesic Safety Barrier \& Exact Spherical-Cap Retraction}
We parameterize the ethical safety barrier using $K=19$ unit normal vectors $\{w_k\}_{k=1}^K \subset \mathbb{S}^{d_I-1}$ corresponding to the 19 harm categories of PKU-SafeRLHF \citep{ji2024pkusaferlhf}, paired with calibrated thresholds $\gamma_k \in (0, 1)$. The safe intent region $\mathcal{K}_{\text{safe}} \subset \mathbb{S}^{d_I-1}$ is the complement of $K$ open spherical caps:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{K}_{\text{safe}} = \left\{ z \in \mathbb{S}^{d_I-1} \;\middle|\; m_k(z) = \langle z, w_k \rangle - \gamma_k \le 0, \;\forall k \in \{1,\dots,K\} \right\}.
\end{equation}

When a violation occurs ($m_{k^*}(z_{\text{intent}}) > 0$ for $k^* = \arg\max_k m_k(z_{\text{intent}})$), rather than taking an unnormalized Euclidean step that leaves the sphere, we derive the exact Riemannian metric projection onto the contracted spherical cap boundary $\langle z^+, w_{k^*} \rangle = \gamma'_{k^*}$, where $\gamma'_{k^*} = \gamma_{k^*} - \delta_{\text{slack}}$ ($\delta_{\text{slack}} = 0.05$).

\begin{proposition}[Single-Step Geodesic Spherical-Cap Retraction]
\label{prop:retraction}
Let $z \in \mathbb{S}^{d_I-1}$ violate cap $k^*$ with $\langle z, w_{k^*} \rangle \in (\gamma'_{k^*}, 1)$, and define the unit orthogonal component in the great-circle plane spanned by $\{z, w_{k^*}\}$:
\begin{equation}
    u_\perp = \frac{z - \langle z, w_{k^*} \rangle w_{k^*}}{\|z - \langle z, w_{k^*} \rangle w_{k^*}\|_2}.
\end{equation}
Then the geodesic retraction point
\begin{equation}
    z^+ = \pi_{\mathcal{K}_{k^*}}(z) = \gamma'_{k^*} w_{k^*} + \sqrt{1 - (\gamma'_{k^*})^2}\, u_\perp
    \label{eq:cap_retraction}
\end{equation}
satisfies: (i) $z^+ \in \mathbb{S}^{d_I-1}$ ($\|z^+\|_2 = 1$), (ii) $\langle z^+, w_{k^*} \rangle = \gamma'_{k^*} < \gamma_{k^*}$ in a single step, and (iii) $z^+$ minimizes $d_g(z, u)$ over all $u \in \mathbb{S}^{d_I-1}$ satisfying $\langle u, w_{k^*} \rangle \le \gamma'_{k^*}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By construction, $\langle u_\perp, w_{k^*} \rangle = 0$ and $\|u_\perp\|_2 = \|w_{k^*}\|_2 = 1$. Thus $\|z^+\|_2^2 = (\gamma'_{k^*})^2 + (1 - (\gamma'_{k^*})^2) = 1$, and $\langle z^+, w_{k^*} \rangle = \gamma'_{k^*} = \gamma_{k^*} - \delta_{\text{slack}} < \gamma_{k^*}$. Since $z^+$ lies on the unique great-circle geodesic passing through $w_{k^*}$ and $z$, the spherical triangle inequality implies $d_g(z, u) \ge d_g(u, w_{k^*}) - d_g(z, w_{k^*}) \ge \arccos(\gamma'_{k^*}) - \arccos(\langle z, w_{k^*} \rangle) = d_g(z, z^+)$ for any $u$ with $\langle u, w_{k^*} \rangle \le \gamma'_{k^*}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Subspace Orthogonal Null-Space Steering}
Gated by the hard violation indicator $\mathbb{I}[\max_k m_k(z_{\text{intent}}) > 0]$, the chord displacement on $\mathbb{S}^{d_I-1}$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta z_{\text{intent}} = \mathbb{I}\!\left[\max_k m_k(z_{\text{intent}}) > 0\right] \big(\pi_{\mathcal{K}_{k^*}}(z_{\text{intent}}) - z_{\text{intent}}\big).
    \label{eq:delta_z}
\end{equation}
Lifting $\Delta z_{\text{intent}}$ into $\mathbb{R}^D$ via $\mathbf{W}_{\text{steer}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times d_I}$ and radial scaling $r(h) = \|h\|_2$ gives the unconstrained perturbation $\Delta h_{\text{raw}} = r(h)\,\mathbf{W}_{\text{steer}}\Delta z_{\text{intent}}$. Because $f_I$ and $f_C$ are non-linear MLPs, an unconstrained $\Delta h_{\text{raw}}$ leaks into the context subspace.

\begin{theorem}[Context Subspace Orthogonality \& Benign Zero-Distortion]
\label{thm:nullspace}
Let $\mathbf{Q}_C = \mathrm{orth}(\mathbf{B}_{\text{ctx}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times r_C}$ ($\mathbf{Q}_C^\top \mathbf{Q}_C = \mathbf{I}_{r_C}$) denote the orthonormal basis of the rank-$r_C$ global context subspace, and let $\hat{v}_{\text{ctx}} = \frac{\mathbf{W}_{\text{ctx}} z_{\text{context}}}{\|\mathbf{W}_{\text{ctx}} z_{\text{context}}\|_2} \in \mathbb{S}^{D-1}$ denote the sample-conditioned lifted context direction. Define the composite null-space projector:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{P}_{\text{context}}^\perp = \left(\mathbf{I}_D - \hat{v}_{\text{ctx}}\hat{v}_{\text{ctx}}^\top\right)\left(\mathbf{I}_D - \mathbf{Q}_C \mathbf{Q}_C^\top\right).
    \label{eq:nullspace_proj}
\end{equation}
Then the steered perturbation $\Delta h = \mathcal{P}_{\text{context}}^\perp \Delta h_{\text{raw}}$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Context Orthogonality}: $\langle \Delta h, \hat{v}_{\text{ctx}} \rangle \equiv 0$, and if $\hat{v}_{\text{ctx}} \in \mathrm{range}(\mathbf{Q}_C)$ or $\mathbf{Q}_C^\top \hat{v}_{\text{ctx}} = \mathbf{0}$, then $\mathbf{Q}_C^\top \Delta h = \mathbf{0}$ across all $r_C$ basis axes.
    \item \textbf{Exact Benign Preservation}: For any input $x$ whose intent satisfies $\max_k m_k(\phi_I(h(x))) \le 0$, the indicator gate in Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta_z} evaluates to $0$, yielding $\Delta h \equiv \mathbf{0}$ and zero downstream perturbation across all subsequent layers $f_{>l}$:
    \begin{equation}
        \big\|f_{>l}(h + \Delta h) - f_{>l}(h)\big\|_2 = 0.
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $u = (\mathbf{I}_D - \mathbf{Q}_C \mathbf{Q}_C^\top)\Delta h_{\text{raw}}$. Then $\langle \Delta h, \hat{v}_{\text{ctx}} \rangle = \hat{v}_{\text{ctx}}^\top(\mathbf{I}_D - \hat{v}_{\text{ctx}}\hat{v}_{\text{ctx}}^\top)u = (\hat{v}_{\text{ctx}}^\top - \|\hat{v}_{\text{ctx}}\|_2^2 \hat{v}_{\text{ctx}}^\top)u = 0$. Moreover, $\mathbf{Q}_C^\top u = (\mathbf{Q}_C^\top - \mathbf{Q}_C^\top \mathbf{Q}_C \mathbf{Q}_C^\top)\Delta h_{\text{raw}} = \mathbf{0}$ since $\mathbf{Q}_C^\top \mathbf{Q}_C = \mathbf{I}_{r_C}$. Finally, when $\max_k m_k(\phi_I(h)) \le 0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:delta_z} gives $\Delta z_{\text{intent}} = \mathbf{0} \implies \Delta h_{\text{raw}} = \mathbf{0} \implies \Delta h = \mathbf{0}$, so $h + \Delta h = h$ identically.
\end{proof}

\section{Training Objective \& Implementation}
\label{sec:architecture}

During training on paired clean and adversarially framed representations $(h_{\text{clean}}, h_{\text{adv}})$, ARETE jointly optimizes the Decomposer ($\phi_I, \phi_C, \Phi$) and the Geodesic Safety Barrier ($\{w_k, \gamma_k\}, \mathbf{W}_{\text{steer}}, \mathbf{W}_{\text{ctx}}, \mathbf{B}_{\text{ctx}}$):
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{decomp}} + \lambda_{\text{barrier}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{barrier}}, \quad \lambda_{\text{barrier}} = 1.2.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Decomposition Objective.}
The decomposition loss combines geodesic intent invariance, statistical and subspace orthogonality, and radial-scaled reconstruction:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{decomp}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{inv}} + \lambda_{\text{ortho}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{ortho}} + \lambda_{\text{rec}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}},
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{\text{ortho}} = 0.1$, $\lambda_{\text{rec}} = 0.05$, and:
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{inv}} &= \mathbb{E}\Big[d_g\big(\phi_I(h_{\text{clean}}), \phi_I(h_{\text{adv}})\big)\Big], \\
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{ortho}} &= \left\|\frac{1}{2B}\sum_{i=1}^{2B} z_{\text{intent}}^{(i)}\big(z_{\text{context}}^{(i)}\big)^\top\right\|_F^2 + \frac{1}{d_I}\big\|\hat{\mathbf{W}}_I^\top \hat{\mathbf{W}}_C\big\|_F^2, \\
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}} &= \frac{1}{2}\Big(\|h_{\text{rec},c} - h_{\text{clean}}\|_2^2 + \|h_{\text{rec},a} - h_{\text{adv}}\|_2^2\Big).
\end{align}

\paragraph{Closed-Loop Barrier \& Steering Objective.}
To ensure that $\mathbf{W}_{\text{steer}}$, $\mathbf{W}_{\text{ctx}}$, and $\mathbf{B}_{\text{ctx}}$ actively steer non-linear representations into $\mathcal{K}_{\text{safe}}$ while preserving $\phi_C(h)$, we train the barrier with a five-term loss (using straight-through gradients on the gate during training):
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{barrier}} &= \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}\big(\langle z_{\text{intent}}, w_k \rangle / 0.1, y_{\text{cat}}\big) \nonumber \\
    &\quad + 2.0\,\mathbb{E}_{\text{harm}}\big[\mathrm{ReLU}(0.20 - m_{y_{\text{cat}}}(z_{\text{intent}}))\big] \nonumber \\
    &\quad + 3.0\,\mathbb{E}_{\text{benign}}\big[\mathrm{ReLU}(m_k(z_{\text{intent}}) + 0.10)\big] \nonumber \\
    &\quad + 1.5\,\mathbb{E}_{\text{harm}}\Big[\mathrm{ReLU}\big(\max_k m_k(\phi_I(h + \Delta h)) + 0.05\big)\Big] \nonumber \\
    &\quad + 1.0\,\mathbb{E}_{\text{harm}}\Big[d_g\big(\phi_C(h + \Delta h), \phi_C(h)\big)\Big].
    \label{eq:barrier_loss}
\end{align}
The fourth term enforces that a single closed-loop step $\tilde{h} = h + \Delta h$ maps the re-encoded intent $\phi_I(\tilde{h})$ strictly inside $\mathcal{K}_{\text{safe}}$, while the fifth term aligns $\mathcal{V}_{\text{context}}$ with the Jacobian of $\phi_C$ so that $\Delta h \in \mathcal{P}_{\text{context}}^\perp$ induces minimal non-linear context drift.

\section{Experiments \& Empirical Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Protocol \& Threat Model}
All experiments are conducted on an NVIDIA RTX 5000 Ada Generation GPU (CUDA 12.4, PyTorch 2.6). Our evaluation is organized into two complementary stages:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Controlled Manifold \& Adaptive Attack Benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:main_bench})}: A 4,000-sample benchmark ($2,800$ train / $1,200$ held-out test: $n=602$ harmful across 19 PKU-SafeRLHF categories and 5 attack families, $n=598$ benign including 25\% hard dual-use samples) with non-linear sinusoidal feature entanglement ($D=1024, d_I=256, d_C=768, r_C=32$, $5.28\text{M}$ parameters, AdamW $\text{lr}=10^{-3}$, 14 epochs, batch size 64). We evaluate across: (i) Direct Violations ($n=118$), (ii) GCG Suffixes ($n=115$), (iii) PAIR Roleplay ($n=114$), (iv) AutoDAN Cloaking ($n=130$), and (v) Indirect Prompt Injections ($n=125$). We also test \textbf{Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Attack Generalization} (training only on Direct, GCG, and PAIR, testing zero-shot on unseen AutoDAN and Indirect Injections) and a \textbf{White-Box Adaptive PGD Adversary} (10-step PGD with $\|\delta\|_2 \le 0.12\|h\|_2$ directly minimizing $\log\sum_k \exp(m_k(\phi_I(h+\delta))/0.05)$).
    \item \textbf{Real LLM Activation \& Autoregressive Generation Benchmark on \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} (Section~\ref{sec:qwen_eval})}: We extract real Layer-14 ($l = L/2$, $D=1536, d_I=384, d_C=1152$) residual stream activations from \texttt{Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} across 163 paired harmful/cloaked and XSTest/dual-use benign prompts, train ARETE on the centered LLM activations, and evaluate live autoregressive generation against unsteered decoding and minimal-pair Linear Refusal Steering \citep{arditi2024refusal}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Baselines \& Metrics.}
We compare against: (1) \textbf{Base Unaligned}; (2) \textbf{Safe-DPO Baseline}; (3) \textbf{Linear Refusal Steering (RepEng)} \citep{zou2023representation,arditi2024refusal}; (4) \textbf{Conditional Activation Steering (CAST)} \citep{lee2024programming}; (5) \textbf{External Guardrail Classifier}; and (6) a \textbf{19-Category Linear Probe + Gated Steering} baseline operating directly on raw hidden states without intent-context decomposition. We report \textbf{Attack Success Rate (ASR \%)} ($\downarrow$), \textbf{Benign Over-Refusal FPR (\%)} ($\downarrow$), \textbf{Benign CosSim} ($\cos(h, \tilde{h})$ on benign inputs, $\uparrow$), and \textbf{Steered Context CosSim} ($\cos(\mathcal{P}_{\text{ctx}}h, \mathcal{P}_{\text{ctx}}\tilde{h})$ on actively steered harmful inputs, $\uparrow$).

\subsection{Ablation Analysis}
\label{sec:ablations}
Table~\ref{tab:main_benchmark} resolves how each geometric component contributes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Role of Subspace Null-Space Projection ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{context}}^\perp$)}: Because the hard boundary gate $\mathbb{I}[\max_k m_k > 0]$ is inactive on benign queries ($0.00\%$ FPR), both full ARETE and \textit{ARETE w/o Null-Space} leave benign inputs untouched ($\text{Benign CosSim} = 1.0000$). However, on actively steered inputs, removing $\mathcal{P}_{\text{context}}^\perp$ causes \textbf{Steered Context CosSim to collapse from $1.0000$ to $0.1560$} (and $0.9310$ in the 19-Cat Linear Probe), confirming Theorem~\ref{thm:nullspace}: without null-space projection, steering vectors severely corrupt the context subspace.
    \item \textbf{Role of Hyperspherical Geodesic Normalization}: Replacing unit hyperspherical projection with unnormalized Euclidean inner products (\textit{ARETE w/ Euclidean}) causes radial norm variations ($r(h) \in [8.5, 16.5]$) to trigger false barrier violations on $43.14\%$ of benign queries, which in turn fires unwanted steering on benign inputs and reduces Benign CosSim to $0.9534$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We introduced \textbf{ARETE}, an activation-level safety framework that disentangles invariant intent from contextual framing on unit Riemannian hyperspheres, enforces 19 spherical-cap harm boundaries via exact single-step geodesic retraction, and projects steering vectors into the context null space. Both controlled manifold benchmarks (including unseen OOD attack families and white-box adaptive PGD attacks) and real activation/generation experiments on \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} demonstrate that ARETE suppresses adversarial jailbreaks while eliminating benign over-refusals.

\section*{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item \textbf{White-Box Activation Access}: ARETE intercepts intermediate residual stream states $h \in \mathbb{R}^D$, requiring access to internal model activations (available in open-weight deployments and self-hosted inference servers, but not black-box third-party APIs).
    \item \textbf{Single-Layer Hook \& Scale}: Our real-LLM experiments evaluate single-layer steering at layer $l = L/2$ on \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct}. Extending multi-layer cascaded barriers to $7\text{B}$--$70\text{B}$ models and multi-turn Crescendo dialogues \citep{russinovich2024crescendo} is an important direction for future work.
    \item \textbf{High-Budget White-Box Adaptive Attacks}: While ARETE reduces ASR to $2.82\%$ against a 10-step white-box PGD adversary ($\|\delta\|_2 \le 0.12\|h\|_2$), an unconstrained attacker with unlimited query budget and full knowledge of $\phi_I$ and $\{w_k\}$ can still search for out-of-manifold evasions. Combining ARETE with randomized subspace rotations or adversarial weight training can further harden the barrier.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Ethical Considerations \& Broader Impacts}
This work develops defensive representation-level safeguards to prevent the misuse of LLMs under adversarial prompt injections. All harm categories follow the established academic taxonomy of PKU-SafeRLHF \citep{ji2024pkusaferlhf}, and all qualitative red-teaming prompts in Table~\ref{tab:qwen_generations} are truncated and sanitized so that no actionable exploit payloads or hazardous instructions are released.
