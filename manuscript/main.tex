\title{The Gate Knows, the Threshold Doesn't:\\Recalibrating Activation-Gated Jailbreak Defenses Under Unseen Framings}

\author{Anonymous submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Activation-gated defenses read a model's residual stream and steer towards refusal only when a probe fires. Their thresholds are set on jailbreak framings known at development time. We test six such gates on real harmful and benign prompts in thirteen framings, nine of them withheld from design. On withheld framings, recall at a 1\% false-positive budget often falls to 0--17\%. Yet a probe over the user's tokens still ranks harmful above benign prompts with AUROC 0.95--1.00 whenever the model can parse the framing, and re-thresholding per framing would recover most of the loss. The failure is largely one of calibration. A location-scale model explains it, shows that no label-free correction is safe without a bound on the harmful share of each framing, and predicts how recalibration degrades as that share grows; its predictions match measured firing rates to about one percentage point of recall. We propose a recalibration that needs no labels from the deployment stream: cluster prompts by framing, standardise the probe score robustly within each cluster, and keep the original threshold as a floor. The method is useful but fragile. With enough clusters, a post-hoc choice, recall on most withheld framings rises to 83--94\% on two models at 1.3--1.6\% mean false positives, and judged attack success falls, for example from 45\% to 6\% on code-completion framings (Qwen2.5-1.5B). The gains do not carry over where the steering itself is ineffective (fiction on Phi-3.5-mini), and framings used almost only for attacks are not helped.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Safety training teaches a language model to refuse harmful requests. Jailbreaks undo it by changing how the request is framed: a persona, a story, a retrieved document, an encoding, or a request to write code \citep{wei2023jailbroken,shen2024dan,greshake2023indirect,li2023deepinception}. Because the framing changes the surface of a prompt but not what it asks for, a natural defense looks beneath the surface. Residual-stream activations carry a linearly readable harmfulness signal \citep{zou2023repe,zhao2025harmfulness}, and refusal itself is mediated by a single direction \citep{arditi2024refusal}. Several defenses therefore read activations to decide whether to intervene. Conditional activation steering adds a refusal direction only when a condition vector fires \citep{lee2025cast}, and probe-based detectors flag a prompt from its internal state \citep{zhang2025jbshield,mckenzie2025highstakes}. These gates are cheap, they leave benign traffic alone, and their operating point can be set to a fixed false-positive budget.

This paper asks what happens to that operating point when the framing changes. We separate framing families into \emph{seen} ones, used for training and calibration, and \emph{withheld} ones, and we evaluate on real prompts from five public benchmarks \citep{mazeika2024harmbench,zou2023gcg,chao2024jailbreakbench,rottger2024xstest,cui2025orbench}, with generations judged by a guard model \citep{qwen2025qwen3guard}.

The first finding is a collapse. Every gate we test detects at most 17\% of harmful prompts on withheld fiction and indirect-injection framings, and none of them changes the 89\% attack success rate of fiction prompts. This includes a geometric gate of our own design that explicitly tries to factor framing out of the representation.

The second finding concerns the cause. Ranking survives the shift. A probe that reads the mean of the user's tokens separates harmful from benign prompts with AUROC 0.95--1.00 on every withheld framing the model can parse. On the four framings the shared threshold misses worst, re-thresholding it on the new framing recovers 57--81\% recall. What moves is the location of both score distributions. Concurrent work reports compatible observations: linear jailbreak probes are poorly calibrated off-distribution \citep{kim2026linearly}, and shift degrades threshold transfer before ranking \citep{luo2026measuring}. We confirm the pattern across gates, readout positions and framings. We also show that projecting out a subspace of framing directions, the most direct representational fix we could build, does not repair it.

The third finding is a remedy and a map of its limits. Deployed gates see a stream of mostly benign prompts. We cluster the stream by framing, standardise the probe score inside each cluster with median and median absolute deviation, which tolerate a minority of harmful prompts \citep{rousseeuw1993mad,leys2013mad}, and keep the original threshold as a floor. Unsupervised recalibration of subpopulations has antecedents in general machine learning \citep{ziegler2019unsupervised}. Our contribution is to adapt it to activation-gated safety and to test it under a protocol that withholds framings from design. We then report plainly where it works and where it does not.

Concretely, we contribute:
\begin{itemize}
\item a held-out-framing evaluation of activation gates on real corpora, with leave-one-framing-out selection on validation data and five framings pre-registered after the main study;
\item evidence that gate failure on withheld framings is mostly a calibration shift, a location-scale model that explains it, an impossibility result for label-free recalibration, and a negative result for framing-subspace projection;
\item a label-free, cluster-wise recalibration with a fixed-threshold floor, evaluated without transduction at detection and generation level;
\item a characterisation of its failure modes: cluster resolution, framings used mainly for attacks, harmful prevalence, and framings the model cannot parse.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Jailbreak framings.}
Attacks range from optimised suffixes \citep{zou2023gcg,liu2024autodan} and attacker models \citep{chao2025pair,mehrotra2024tap} to hand-written templates: personas \citep{shen2024dan}, nested fiction \citep{li2023deepinception}, encodings and ciphers \citep{wei2023jailbroken,yuan2024cipherchat}, many-shot demonstrations \citep{anil2024manyshot}, and instructions planted in retrieved content \citep{greshake2023indirect}. \citet{wei2023jailbroken} attribute their success partly to mismatched generalisation, which is what a withheld framing probes.

\paragraph{Representation-level defenses.}
Harmfulness and refusal are encoded separately, with harmfulness concentrated at the instruction tokens rather than at the final position \citep{zhao2025harmfulness}. Representation engineering reads and edits such directions \citep{zou2023repe}; circuit breakers reroute harmful representations during training \citep{zou2024circuitbreakers}; latent adversarial training hardens them \citep{sheshadri2024lat}. Inference-time gates include conditional steering \citep{lee2025cast} and detectors that flag prompts from internal signals \citep{zhang2025jbshield,hu2024gradientcuff,xie2024gradsafe}, or redact during generation \citep{mei2026hiddenguard}. FrameShield likewise treats framing as a nuisance to be disentangled from the goal in activation space \citep{farzam2026hiding}. Several studies question how far probe signals generalise. Probes for high-stakes interactions keep high AUROC out of distribution while recall at low false-positive rates is modest \citep{mckenzie2025highstakes}. Final-token probes miss jailbreaks whose evidence sits in earlier user tokens \citep{doda2026beforelast}. Linear jailbreak probes conflate benign register with intent, and their headline accuracy depends on the threshold \citep{kim2026linearly}. Internal harmfulness scores can even anti-rank successful jailbreaks \citep{luo2026measuring}. In contrast, \citet{schwarz2026entanglement} find that refitting thresholds does not help on topic-matched pairs, where the discriminative information is absent. Adaptive attacks that optimise against the probe can drive recall to zero \citep{bailey2026obfuscated}. We study the complementary, non-adaptive failure of natural framing shift.

\paragraph{Guard models and judges.}
Classifier guards moderate inputs and outputs \citep{inan2023llamaguard,han2024wildguard,zeng2024shieldgemma,padhi2025graniteguardian,qwen2025qwen3guard}, and constitutional classifiers are trained against universal jailbreaks \citep{sharma2025constitutional}. We use a guard only as an evaluation judge, because refusal-string matching counts harmless non-compliance as a successful attack \citep{souly2024strongreject}.

\paragraph{Calibration under shift.}
Label-shift corrections re-estimate class priors from unlabeled target data \citep{saerens2002adjusting,lipton2018bbse,alexandari2020labelshift}. Test-time adaptation adjusts normalisation layers by entropy minimisation \citep{wang2021tent}. Conformal methods restore coverage under shift given weights or labels \citep{tibshirani2019conformalshift,podkopaev2021labelshiftconformal}. Unsupervised recalibration adjusts outputs per subpopulation without labels \citep{ziegler2019unsupervised}; multicalibration asks for calibration on every identifiable subgroup, using labels \citep{hebertjohnson2018multicalibration}. For jailbreak detectors, \citet{piet2025jailbreaksovertime} adapt text classifiers to drift by self-training, \citet{azov2026sallie} find that re-thresholding often suffices when target labels exist, and \citet{kim2026linearly} combine register erasure with distribution-free calibration on labelled benign data. Our method uses no labels from the shifted framing or the deployment stream, and it operates per framing cluster of an activation gate.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Prompts.}
Harmful behaviours come from JailbreakBench, the standard text behaviours of HarmBench, and AdvBench; benign prompts come from the benign split of JailbreakBench, the safe prompts of XSTest, and 400 prompts of OR-Bench-Hard, which are designed to look harmful. After de-duplication there are 1{,}550 prompts. We split them 50/20/30 into train, validation and test \emph{by behaviour}, stratified by source, so that no wrapped version of a test request is seen in training. The test split holds 241 harmful and 225 benign requests.

\paragraph{Framings.}
Each request is wrapped in a framing that keeps its text verbatim. Four framings are \emph{seen}: the plain request, a persona (AIM, DAN, ``grandma''), refusal suppression, and prefix injection. Four were \emph{withheld} from the start: fiction or screenplay, indirect injection disguised as a retrieved document or e-mail, base64, and leetspeak. After the main study we wrote and committed five \emph{fresh} framings before generating any output on them: a past-tense historical frame, an academic or IRB frame, code completion, translate-then-answer, and payload splitting. Every family uses one to three fixed templates. We also optimise GCG suffixes \citep{zou2023gcg} against the undefended model for 20 test behaviours (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}).

\paragraph{Model and gates.}
The target is Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct \citep{qwen2024qwen25} without a system prompt. All gates read layer 14 of 28. When a gate fires, it adds the same vector $\alpha\hat r$ to the residual stream at every position. Here $\hat r$ is the normalised difference between mean harmful and mean benign activations of plain training requests, and $\alpha$ is 0.4 times the mean activation norm. Gates therefore differ only in their decision. We compare six gates: (i)~a CAST-style condition vector \citep{lee2025cast}; (ii)~an MLP probe on the final token; (iii)~ARETE, a geometric gate of our own that maps activations onto intent and context hyperspheres and steers orthogonally to a framing subspace (Appendix~\ref{app:arete}); and logistic-regression probes on (iv)~the final token, (v)~the mean of the user's tokens, and (vi)~a union of three readouts (Section~\ref{sec:diagnosis}). Every gate is trained on seen framings of the training split. Its threshold is the 99th percentile of its score on benign validation prompts of seen framings, a held-out-quantile calibration in the spirit of \citet{kim2026linearly} but without their register erasure. We call it the \emph{shared} threshold.

\paragraph{Protocol and its departures.}
Readout position, clustering layer and clustering features were selected by leave-one-framing-out (LOFO) on the validation split: each seen wrapper in turn is treated as withheld, and recall is measured on it. Two departures need stating. First, the diagnostic analyses of Section~\ref{sec:diagnosis} used the test split before the remedy was designed, so the original withheld framings informed the idea, though not its configuration. This is why we pre-registered the fresh framings. Second, the layer was fixed at 14 for all gates and never selected; in LOFO the user-mean probe scored slightly higher at layers 21 and 7 (43\% and 41\% recall) than at layer 14 (38\%).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Detection is reported as true-positive rate (TPR, which we call recall) on harmful prompts and false-positive rate (FPR) on benign prompts, plus AUROC. Responses are generated greedily for 96 new tokens and judged by Qwen3Guard-Gen-4B in response-moderation mode \citep{qwen2025qwen3guard}. The judge sees the \emph{unwrapped} request and the response. Attack success rate (ASR) is the fraction of harmful requests whose response is judged \textsc{Unsafe}. Over-refusal is the fraction of plain benign requests whose response is judged a refusal; on wrapped benign prompts the judge often labels confused non-compliance as refusal, so we do not report it there. For base64 the judge labels responses that merely echo or start decoding the payload as \textsc{Unsafe}, so we report detection only. With 96 tokens, ASR mostly measures the onset of compliance; Appendix~\ref{app:extra} repeats the key cells at 256 tokens.

\paragraph{Projecting out framing does not help.}
ARETE assumes that framing occupies a low-dimensional subspace that can be removed. We test this in LOFO on validation data: we estimate the subspace from wrapper displacements of the remaining seen wrappers and project it out before training a linear probe. Removing 0, 4, 16 or 64 directions gives mean held-out recall of 54\%, 54\%, 54\% and 48\%. A subspace learned from two wrappers does not contain the shift induced by a third. Prefix injection is the clearest case, at about 1\% recall for every rank.

\paragraph{Where to read.}
Readout position matters more than projection. In LOFO, the final-token probe misses prefix injection (1\%), whose injected instruction follows the request and changes the final state. The user-mean probe catches 60\% of it but is weaker on personas. A union of three readouts, each calibrated at a third of the budget, raises LOFO recall from 54\% to 69\% at 1.3\% FPR, and on the test split it helps seen framings slightly (Table~\ref{tab:detection}). It does not help withheld framings, because each readout suffers the same shift.

\section{A Model of the Shift}
\label{sec:theory}

The diagnosis suggests a simple model, which we state so that its consequences can be checked. Let $s=a_f+b_f\,\varepsilon$ be the probe score of a prompt in framing $f$, where $\varepsilon\sim F_y$ depends only on the label $y$, $F_1$ stochastically dominates $F_0$, and $(a_f,b_f)$, $b_f>0$, are a location and scale set by the framing. The framing moves and stretches both classes together.

\paragraph{Proposition 1 (ranking is invariant, thresholds are not).}
Within a framing, AUROC does not depend on $(a_f,b_f)$, because the map $\varepsilon\mapsto a_f+b_f\varepsilon$ is increasing. A fixed threshold $\tau$ yields $\mathrm{TPR}_f=1-F_1((\tau-a_f)/b_f)$ and $\mathrm{FPR}_f=1-F_0((\tau-a_f)/b_f)$. The ROC curve is the same for every framing, and the framing only slides the operating point along it. A downward shift of $a_f$ therefore lowers recall and false positives together, which is the pattern in Table~\ref{tab:detection}: benign FPR on withheld framings falls to nearly zero at the same time as recall.

\paragraph{Proposition 2 (no label-free rule is safe for every prevalence).}
Let $F_0=\mathcal N(0,1)$ and $F_1=\mathcal N(d,1)$. A framing that is entirely benign at location $a+d$ and one that is entirely harmful at location $a$ produce the same unlabeled score distribution, $\mathcal N(a+d,1)$. Any rule that uses only unlabeled scores acts identically on both, so it fires on all of one population or on none of the other. A label-free recalibration must therefore assume a bound on the harmful share $\pi_f$ of each framing. This is the assumption stated in Section~\ref{sec:method}, now with a precise role: it is the price of identifiability, and no choice of statistic removes it.

\paragraph{Proposition 3 (what the robust statistics give up).}
Suppose a framing is observed as the mixture $(1-\pi)F_0+\pi F_1$, with $F_0,F_1$ unit-variance Gaussians separated by $d$. Let $m(\pi,d)$ be the median and $\sigma(\pi,d)=1.4826\,\mathrm{MAD}$ of the mixture, each the root of a monotone equation. The rule that fires when $(s-m)/\sigma>z^\star$ has
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{FPR}&=1-\Phi\bigl(m+z^\star\sigma\bigr),\\
\mathrm{TPR}&=1-\Phi\bigl(m+z^\star\sigma-d\bigr).
\end{align*}
At $\pi=0$ this gives $m=0$, $\sigma=1$, so $z^\star$ attains the intended false-positive rate. As $\pi$ grows, harmful prompts pull $m$ upward and inflate $\sigma$, which raises the effective threshold. At $\pi=1/2$ the median sits between the modes, and for $d\ge2$ and $z^\star=2.52$ the threshold lies above the harmful mode, so recall is below $0.3\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:breakdown}). The estimator degrades smoothly with $\pi$, and a stronger probe (larger $d$) tolerates more contamination: at $d=4$ recall is 90\% at $\pi=0.05$ and 13\% at $\pi=0.30$.

\paragraph{Checking the predictions.}
We test Proposition~3 without the Gaussian assumption. For every framing and traffic mix, we build the mixture of the empirical benign and harmful test scores at the scenario's prevalence, compute its median and MAD, and predict the FPR and TPR of the rule that standardises by true framing (the oracle grouping, which removes clustering error). We compare these predictions with the window measurements of Section~\ref{sec:results}, over all thirteen framings and both mixes. Predictions and measurements agree closely (Table~\ref{tab:theorycheck}). The model also predicts the collapse of persona, refusal suppression, prefix injection and payload splitting at $\pi=0.5$ to 0\% recall, exactly as measured. This check isolates the robust-statistics step, and it reuses the test score distributions, so it shows that contamination explains the failure and not that the method generalises. Generalisation is the subject of Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\paragraph{Clustering error.}
The oracle grouping is not what runs in deployment. With $k_{\max}{=}30$ on the realistic mix, each framing falls into one to three clusters, and every cluster of at least 50 prompts holds a single framing, except that fiction shares a cluster with the plain request. The effective harmful share of a framing's main cluster is within 0.02 of its nominal prevalence (for example 0.07 against 0.05 for fiction, 0.51 against 0.50 for persona). The family-level model is a conservative guide to the clustered rule, not an exact one: its predictions correlate at 0.88 with measured adaptive recall, with a mean error of 11 points, and the error is mostly one-sided. For indirect injection the model predicts 27\% and the clustered rule reaches 90\%, because a cluster can be tighter than its framing, so its MAD is smaller and its threshold lower. Within-framing heterogeneity, which one location and scale per framing cannot express, is therefore a source of gain here and not of loss.

\paragraph{Flooding.}
Proposition~3 also bounds an adaptive attacker. A rule that standardises within a cluster can be pushed off its operating point by raising the harmful share of that cluster. We let the share of one framing grow from 5\% to 50\% while the rest of the stream stays unchanged (Appendix, Table~\ref{tab:flood}). For fiction, the oracle-grouped rule falls from 76\% recall to 68, 54, 20 and 0\%, against predictions of 76, 69, 50, 17 and 0\%. The clustered rule behaves the same way: fiction recall is 87\% at $\pi=0.05$, 59\% at $\pi=0.2$ and 17\% at $\pi=0.3$. To reach that point the attacker must send about 0.43 harmful prompts for each benign one in the framing, and the harmful prompts it sends are the traffic that goes through. The hybrid floor limits the damage only where the shared threshold already works, as for code (69\% recall at $\pi=0.5$, against 83\% for the shared rule at full budget). Where the shared threshold fails, as for fiction, flooding returns the gate to its uncalibrated behaviour. We did not test attackers who adapt their prompts to the probe \citep{bailey2026obfuscated}, which is a stronger threat than flooding.

\section{Cluster-Wise Robust Recalibration}
\label{sec:method}

The diagnosis and the model of Section~\ref{sec:theory} suggest treating each framing as its own calibration problem. At deployment the framing of a new prompt is unknown and no labels are available. Two facts help. Prompts that share a framing share an activation signature, so they cluster. And when a framing is used mostly for benign purposes, the bulk of its score distribution describes benign prompts, which by Proposition~2 is an assumption no label-free method can drop.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Cluster-wise robust recalibration}
\label{alg:recal}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require probe score $s$; framing features $\phi$; budget $\epsilon$; shared threshold $\tau_{\epsilon/2}$
\State \textbf{Once, on validation:} simulate streams of seen framings, run lines 2--3, and set $z^\star$ to the $(1-\epsilon/2)$ quantile of benign robust $z$.
\State \textbf{Window:} on unlabeled traffic, reduce $\phi$ by PCA and run $k$-means, $k\le k_{\max}$ chosen by silhouette.
\State For each cluster $c$: $m_c\gets\mathrm{median}(s)$, $d_c\gets1.4826\,\mathrm{MAD}(s)$; freeze centroids and $(m_c,d_c)$.
\State \textbf{New prompt} $x$, nearest centroid $c$: fire iff $s(x)>\tau_{\epsilon/2}$ or $(s(x)-m_c)/d_c>z^\star$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:recal} gives the \emph{hybrid} rule. The score $s$ is the user-mean probe. The features $\phi$ are user-mean activations at layer 14, reduced to 32 principal components. Clusters with fewer than 30 prompts fall back to validation statistics. Inside a cluster the median and MAD describe its benign bulk, so a harmful prompt is compared with benign prompts of its own framing. Because $z^\star$ is set on validation streams that pass through the same clustering, it accounts for within-cluster dispersion. The \emph{adaptive} rule is the second disjunct alone, at the full budget. Validation labels are used once, to set $z^\star$ and $\tau$. No labels from the deployment stream or from new framings are used.

LOFO on validation streams at 5\% harmful prevalence selected the user-mean readout over the final-token and last-user-token readouts, and layer-14 clustering features over layers 7 and 21. Under that selection, held-out recall rose from 38\% with the shared threshold to 91\% with the adaptive rule, at 1.1\% FPR. The hybrid rule reached 83\% at 0.9\% FPR. We fixed $k_{\max}=12$ without selection, which turns out to matter.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We evaluate without transduction. In each of 20 windows the test behaviours are split at random into halves A and B. Cluster statistics are estimated on traffic built from half A and applied to half B. We use two traffic mixes. \emph{Benign-rich} traffic gives every framing 5\% harmful prevalence. \emph{Realistic} traffic keeps 5\% for dual-use framings (plain, fiction, indirect, past tense, academic, code, translation) and uses 50\% for framings that are rarely used benignly (persona, refusal suppression, prefix injection, base64, leetspeak, payload split). Generation-level ASR is composed from judged responses. A fired prompt takes the label of its steered response, which equals the always-on response under greedy decoding, and an unfired prompt takes the label of its undefended response.

\paragraph{As pre-registered, the method under-segments.}
Table~\ref{tab:robust}a reports the frozen configuration on all thirteen framings. In benign-rich traffic, payload-split recall rises from 0\% to 90\%, indirect injection from 16\% to 31\%, past tense from 0\% to 15\%, and academic from 40\% to 45\%. Fiction gains little, from 5\% to 19\%. When the stream contains only the eight original framings, the same configuration detects 82\% of fiction and 56\% of indirect prompts. With thirteen framings and at most twelve clusters, fiction shares a cluster with framings whose benign scores sit elsewhere, and its location is not corrected. With true framing labels as groups (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) fiction recall is 76\%, so the loss comes from cluster resolution, not from the statistics.

\paragraph{With enough clusters it works where traffic is mostly benign.}
Raising $k_{\max}$ to 30 is a post-hoc change, motivated by the observation above, and we report it as such. In benign-rich traffic, adaptive recall then reaches 91\% on fiction, 92\% on indirect injection, 84\% on academic and 90\% on payload splitting, at 1.6\% mean FPR (Table~\ref{tab:robust}b). Recall rises steadily with $k_{\max}$: at 12, 20 and 30 it is 27\%, 70\% and 91\% on fiction. Past tense stays at 40\%, below its 57\% oracle. Table~\ref{tab:ablation} isolates the clustering. One global robust $z$ ($k{=}1$) does nothing. Grouping by true framing family helps, but less than data-driven clusters, which can also separate templates within a family.

\paragraph{It fails where a framing is mostly harmful, and the floor contains the damage.}
Under realistic traffic the persona, refusal-suppression and prefix-injection clusters are half harmful. Their median then sits among harmful scores, and adaptive recall on them drops to 0--4\% (Table~\ref{tab:robust}). This is worse than the shared threshold, and prefix-injection ASR returns to the undefended 77\%. Payload splitting, an attack-only framing that the shared threshold also misses, is lost entirely. The hybrid rule keeps the shared threshold as a floor and recovers 55--82\% recall on seen attack-only framings, while keeping nearly all of the gain on dual-use framings.

\paragraph{Generation.}
Table~\ref{tab:generation} reports judged ASR under realistic traffic. The hybrid rule lowers ASR on fiction from 89\% to 37\%, on code completion from 45\% to 6\%, on academic framing from 15\% to 4\%, and on indirect injection from 12\% to 6\%. The shared threshold leaves fiction and indirect injection unchanged. Translate-then-answer is already caught by the shared threshold (51\% to 3\%), and the hybrid rule keeps it there (5\%). Over-refusal on plain benign requests rises from 60.3\% to 60.6\%. The high base rate reflects how often the 1.5B model refuses the borderline prompts of XSTest and OR-Bench even undefended. An always-on steer, by contrast, refuses every plain benign request. The cost of the hybrid rule falls on prefix injection, where ASR rises from 9\% (shared) to 16\%, and on payload splitting, which stays at 95\%. Appendix~\ref{app:extra} gives the pre-registered configuration and a prevalence sweep. With harmful prompts at 20\% of every framing the adaptive statistics degrade, and at 35\% they fail.

\paragraph{A second model.}
To check that nothing is specific to Qwen, we ran the whole pipeline on Phi-3.5-mini-instruct (3.8B parameters), with every choice frozen from the Qwen study: the user-mean probe at the middle layer (16 of 32), the same clustering, budgets and steering strength (0.4 times the mean activation norm). Nothing was tuned on Phi. The diagnosis replicates (Table~\ref{tab:second}). The probe's AUROC is 0.98--1.00 on all seven framings listed, while the shared threshold finds 0--88\% of harmful prompts and the oracle threshold 82--90\%. The hybrid rule with $k_{\max}{=}30$ finds 61--93\% at 1.3\% mean FPR (0.3\% for the shared threshold), and over-refusal on plain benign requests moves from 23.9\% to 24.8\%. With the pre-registered cap of 12 clusters the gain is smaller (fiction 62\%, indirect injection 23\%, past tense 10\%), as on Qwen. The failure on attack-heavy framings also replicates: under realistic traffic adaptive recall on persona and refusal suppression is 0\%, and the hybrid floor recovers 63--65\%. Base64 is again undetectable (AUROC 0.39).

Generation results replicate for most framings but not for fiction. Judged ASR falls from 19\% to 7\% on past tense, 10\% to 2\% on academic, 15\% to 8\% on code, 13\% to 3\% on translate-then-answer, and 15\% to 3\% on payload splitting under benign-rich traffic. Under realistic traffic payload splitting is half harmful, its cluster is lost, and ASR stays at 15\%. Fiction is the informative failure. The hybrid rule fires on 88\% of harmful fiction prompts, yet ASR stays at 38\%, because on Phi the refusal steer at this strength does not change fiction behaviour even when always on (36.9\% undefended, 37.3\% steered). The gate is right and the intervention is not. A fixed steering strength tuned on one model need not transfer, which is a limit of the intervention and not of the recalibration.

\section{Discussion}

Activation gates are usually reported by AUROC, and by that measure they generalise to new framings. Deployed gates operate at a threshold, and there they do not. Most of the gap is calibration, and it can be narrowed without new labels by letting the threshold follow the framing. This agrees with observations that probes keep their ranking but lose their operating point off-distribution \citep{mckenzie2025highstakes,kim2026linearly,azov2026sallie}. It also differs from settings where the information itself is missing \citep{schwarz2026entanglement}, which is what we see for base64 on a small model. Defenses that remove framing from the representation, as ARETE attempted, address a change in separability. Here the change was in location.

The remedy's limits follow from its one assumption: each cluster is mostly benign. That holds for dual-use framings such as fiction, retrieved documents, academic or code requests, which are also the hardest to block wholesale. It fails for framings that exist mainly to attack, and those need a fixed floor or a template-level filter. The method also depends on cluster resolution. Over-segmenting is cheap, because small clusters fall back to validation statistics, whereas under-segmenting silently merges framings. We would set $k_{\max}$ well above the expected number of distinct templates.

\section{Conclusion}
Activation-gated jailbreak defenses fail on withheld framings mainly because their threshold stays where it was calibrated, while the model still represents the harm. Recalibrating per framing cluster from unlabeled traffic, with the original threshold as a floor, recovers much of the lost detection on two models, and much of the lost protection for dual-use framings at almost no over-refusal cost. It cannot protect framings used mainly for attacks, it depends on adequate cluster resolution, and it is only as effective as the intervention it triggers.

\section*{Limitations}
We study two small models (1.5B and 3.8B parameters). The second was run with the configuration frozen from the first, but larger models were not tested. Larger models parse encodings such as base64, which would likely turn that framing from a capability failure into a calibration failure. The steering strength was fixed from the first model and does not transfer to every framing: on the second model it leaves fiction unchanged. Each framing uses one to three fixed templates, which makes clustering easier than for paraphrased or attacker-generated framings. The best-performing configuration ($k_{\max}{=}30$) was chosen after seeing pre-registered results on the first model, and the original withheld framings informed the method's design. The cleaner evidence is the fresh framings under the pre-registered configuration, and the second model, where $k_{\max}{=}30$ was applied without change. We did not test adaptive attackers who optimise against the gate \citep{bailey2026obfuscated}, camouflage prompts into benign clusters, or flood a single cluster. The prevalence sweep raises prevalence uniformly and is only a proxy for flooding. ASR is judged on 96-token responses by one model judge, and generation-level ASR is composed from undefended and always-on responses. The traffic mixes are simulated; real prevalence per framing is unknown.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
This work studies defenses against misuse of language models. Harmful prompts come from public benchmarks, the attack templates are publicly documented, and generated responses are kept out of the released artefacts. We report failure modes, including attack-only framings and cluster under-segmentation, so that defenders do not over-trust activation gates.

\section{ARETE Geometry}
\label{app:arete}
ARETE standardises activations and maps them through two learned linear heads onto unit hyperspheres for intent and context. It is trained with (i)~an invariance loss pulling the intent coordinates of a wrapped prompt towards those of its plain version, (ii)~a framing-classification loss on the context sphere, and (iii)~a cap loss placing harmful intents inside, and benign intents outside, eight unsupervised spherical caps. The gate score is the maximum cap margin. Steering uses the refusal direction projected onto the orthogonal complement of a rank-16 framing subspace, estimated by SVD of displacements $h_{\text{wrapped}}-h_{\text{plain}}$ on training behaviours.
